Sparse States, Informative Measurements

Real-Time Stereo Event-Inertial Odometry with Gaussian Processes

Mainak Sarkar, Varun Murali
Texas A&M University

Abstract

Event cameras promise robust state estimation where conventional cameras fail, capturing fast motion without blur across a wide range of lighting conditions. Running such estimation in real time on embedded hardware, however, remains challenging, and existing real-time estimators discard measurements to keep up, without regard to what these measurements would contribute.

We present a stereo event-inertial odometry system with a continuous-time Gaussian-process (GP) backend that selects them instead. A new state is placed only when the previous backend update has finished, so that the spacing of the states follows the available computation. The measurements that arrive between two states are scored by their expected information gain on the trajectory, computed from the covariance predicted by the GP motion prior before the update is solved. Under a per-update budget, an informative subset is then sampled across the whole interval, and each selected measurement is incorporated at its own timestamp through GP interpolation.

On six public datasets, our system is the most accurate event-based method on most benchmarks, both offline and in real time on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, where it reduces the mean error of the state-of-the-art AERO-VIS by 20 to 36%. Ablation studies show that using all in-between measurements breaks real-time operation, and that the uncertainty-driven selection matches or improves on random selection on every dataset, with up to 17% lower mean error.

What the selection chooses

The video shows the candidates of individual frames, colored by their expected information gain, and the measurements that the selection draws from them. On slider_fast, whose scene has a distinct foreground and background, the measurements of the near landmarks have a higher gain and are selected more often. On drone_8_hdr, the relation between the depth of a landmark and its gain becomes stronger with the time since the previous state.

Two sequences of the EvSLAM dataset, left camera, with subtitles.

Discard and All against our selection

Discard drops the frames between two states, and All adds every measurement of every frame. Neither bounds the measurements of an update. The videos show the trajectories estimated in real time on VECtor, with one sequence from each of its three environments and the median run of each strategy. Without a budget and a selection method, the estimates of Discard and All drift or lose track, while that of our selection follows the ground truth.

VECtor sequences units-dolly, school-scooter and corridors-dolly.

Random against our selection

Random draws the measurements uniformly, under the same budget as our selection. The two differ in their consistency from run to run, which a single run would not show, so the video overlays five runs of each on drone_8_hdr (EvSLAM). The runs of Random scatter, and one of them leaves the trajectory, while the runs of our selection stay close to each other and to the ground truth.
